The Botanical Profile of the Foxglove

The foxglove, scientifically known as *Digitalis purpurea*, is a flowering plant native to Europe and parts of Asia. It is part of the Plantaginaceae family and is characterized by its striking, tubular flowers that resemble tiny gloves, hence its name. These flowers typically bloom in late spring to early summer, adding a splash of color to woodland floors and garden beds. The plant can grow up to 1.5 meters tall, with lance-shaped leaves that are often covered in a fine, fuzzy texture. The foxglove thrives in moist, well-drained soil and partial to full shade, making it a perfect addition to cottagecore gardens and wildflower meadows. Its presence in the natural world is both a visual and symbolic delight, offering a sense of peace and connection to the earth.
